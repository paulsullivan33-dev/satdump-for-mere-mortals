# The Neighbours

SatDump sits in the middle of a chain: something must listen, something must record, and sometimes something else must present. These are the tools around it, characterised fairly — including the ones that compete with it.

**The radios and their drivers.** A **software-defined radio** is the ear. The [RTL-SDR for Mere Mortals](https://paulsullivan33-dev.github.io/rtl-sdr-for-mere-mortals/) guide covers the thirty-dollar USB receiver that starts most people's satellite habit; SatDump's live mode can drive one directly. Everything upstream of the recording — antennas, amplifiers, the geometry of passes — lives on that side of the fence, and the [Ham Radio for Mere Mortals](https://paulsullivan33-dev.github.io/ham-radio-for-mere-mortals/) guide covers the radio fundamentals.

**The recorders.** **SDR++** and **GQRX** are the listening programs most recordings are made with: they tune the radio, show the spectrum, demodulate for your ears, and — the part that matters here — record baseband or audio to files in the shapes SatDump eats. SDR++ even carries a noise-reduction method for APT audio that SatDump's APT recipe adopts by name. Think of them as the camera and SatDump as the darkroom: many people do both jobs in one sitting, but the crafts are separate, and a recording made well in either program decodes in SatDump regardless of which one made it. Building your own receiver chain instead is the [GNU Radio for Mere Mortals](https://paulsullivan33-dev.github.io/gnu-radio-for-mere-mortals/) guide's territory.

**The other decoders.** **noaa-apt** does one thing — APT — for one constellation, and does it with a fraction of SatDump's bulk; it is also, not coincidentally, the project whose test recording chapter 6 decoded, because a small honest tool's fixtures are gold. Its ancestor WXtoImg defined APT receiving for a generation before being abandoned; SatDump is where most of its users landed. For Meteor LRPT there are dedicated Russian-ecosystem decoders with their own followings. None of this is a rivalry worth having: the recordings are interchangeable, and people routinely decode the same file in two programs to check one against the other — exactly what this guide's lab effectively did.

**The presentation layer.** Decoded folders want an audience. Weather-satellite communities share images through gallery sites and bots that post fresh passes automatically; mapping tools ingest the projected products; and a home server (several guides in this series build them) can host your own archive. SatDump ends at the folder. What the folder becomes is your project, not its problem.

# The Pipeline Catalogue

How much does one program cover? The lab counted, from the installed program's own files (transcript `t01`): **71 pipeline files, 184 pipelines**. This chapter tours the families a reader is most likely to meet, using the names the program itself uses — run a job with the wrong name and SatDump will tell you it doesn't know it, so the names are worth getting from the source, as here.

**NOAA (4 pipelines).** The American polar weather satellites. `noaa_apt` is the 137 MHz analogue picture service — two image channels, slow, forgiving, the beginner's classic, and this guide's worked example. `noaa_hrpt` is the same satellites' fast digital downlink at 1.7 GHz: all five instrument channels, sharp pictures, much more demanding of antenna and receiver. `noaa_gac` and `noaa_dsb` handle recorded dumps and the low-rate beacon.

**Meteor-M (7 pipelines).** Russia's weather series. `meteor_m2_lrpt` and its siblings are the digital picture service at 137 MHz that APT users graduate to: colour images from three channels, but a digital signal that either decodes or doesn't — no graceful static in between. `meteor_hrpt` is its 1.7 GHz counterpart.

**GOES (12 pipelines).** The American geostationary weather satellites, parked over the Americas, sending full-disc images of the Earth on a schedule. The pipelines divide by relay and format (`goes_hrit`, `goes_grb`, `goes_gvar`, and more) because a satellite that never moves has been given many different downlinks over the years. Asia's Himawari and Europe's Meteosat (in `MSG.json`) are the same idea over other longitudes.

**The rest of the sky.** The remaining files cover, among much else: ocean and land observers (Landsat, the EOS and JPSS series, FengYun from China, Elektro and Arktika from Russia), navigation-adjacent and communications relays (Inmarsat, Iridium-era experiments, Orbcomm), deep-space and lunar missions (DSCOVR at the L1 point, Chandrayaan, JUICE on its way to Jupiter), university cubesats by the dozen, and utility recipes filed under `Others.json` — including a `generic_analog_demod` for pulling plain audio out of a baseband recording.

Three honest observations about the catalogue. First, **breadth is not depth**: a pipeline existing means the program knows the format, not that reception is easy — several of these transmissions need dishes, low-noise amplifiers and precise tracking, and the pipeline file will not tell you that. Second, the catalogue is a living thing; pipelines arrive with new satellites and occasionally retire with dead ones, which is why this guide's numbers are dated to the 1.2.2 install the lab counted. Third, a small absence worth naming: amateur-radio **SSTV** (the slow-scan pictures the International Space Station transmits during special events) has **no pipeline in this version's files** — the lab searched the installed catalogue and the program's libraries and found none. If you came to SatDump for an ISS picture event, this build is not your tool; the neighbours chapter names the shape of tool that is.
